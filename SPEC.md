# On Air: what is built

This page describes On Air as it is. [`docs/PRD.md`](docs/PRD.md) describes what it is meant to become.

Milestone one is under way. Two parts exist: a menu-bar recorder that keeps each recording in pieces on disk, and the proved core of the hand-over, through which the recorder hands each finished recording to the folder picked from its menu. Beside them is a first, experimental piece of milestone three, [Live Context](#live-context-a-first-experiment), which hasn't been tried on a Mac yet. The recorder was checked by hand on the owner's Mac on 14 November 2026, and [what was checked and what wasn't](#how-its-checked) is below. None of the milestone's own hand checks in [`docs/PRD.md`](docs/PRD.md#trying-milestone-one-by-hand) has been done in full.

## The recorder

What it does:

- **The menu bar.** A dot, grey when idle and red with a running clock while recording. Its menu has Start or Stop, a note when there is something to say, what to record, where recordings go, Show Live Context, the last recording, the recordings folder and Quit. There is no keyboard shortcut yet.
- **What it records.** What a person picks under Record in the menu: one of the connected displays, listed by name and size, an area of the picked display, or a single window. Until they pick, the whole main display. The picture keeps the shape of what it records, brought down to at most 1080 rows with even sides, at most 15 frames a second, in H.264 at 1.8 Mb/s, with the pointer. The sound and the microphone are mixed into one AAC track, 48 kHz stereo at 128 kb/s. On Air's own sound is left out.
- **Picking.** Picking a display, or "Whole Display", takes effect at the next Start. "Select Area…" dims the picked display and lets a person drag out a rectangle, at least 64 points on each side: Return or letting go of the mouse picks it, and Escape leaves the choice as it was. A line on the dimmed display says so: "Drag to select · Return or let go to confirm · Esc to cancel", near its top, or near its bottom when the rectangle is in the way. "Select Window…" opens the system's window picker, limited to one window. A display or an area is remembered between recordings and launches, in `settings.json` beside the spool, written whole under a hidden name and then renamed. A window lasts until it closes or On Air quits. Nothing can be picked while a recording runs.
- **When the pick isn't there.** If the picked display isn't connected at Start, On Air records the main display; if the area no longer fits its display, the whole display; if the picked window is gone, the whole display. The menu says so while recording and once the recording is saved, and a remembered choice never keeps a recording from starting. A window that closes while it is recorded ends the recording, as capture stopping by itself does: what was recorded is kept, and the menu says why. ScreenCaptureKit says that a window closed from macOS 15.2 on; on earlier versions On Air notices when its menu is next opened.
- **A window's picture and sound.** The picture is the window alone, whatever covers it. Its size is set when the recording starts, from the size the picker's filter gives for the window, which may be the size it had when it was picked. If it is resized, ScreenCaptureKit is asked to scale it to fit that size, keeping its shape. Apple's WWDC22 session "Take ScreenCaptureKit to the next level" says that a single-window filter captures all the sound of the application that owns the window, from its other windows too, and no other application's. The SDK's headers don't say. Recording a window while another covered it was checked by hand. A resized window, and which application's sound a window's recording holds, haven't been tried.
- **Asking for the microphone.** Start asks for the microphone before capture starts. When macOS hasn't asked the person yet, it asks them then, the menu says the recording starts once that question is answered, and it starts when it is. Otherwise macOS answers at once. If the microphone is refused, the recording goes ahead without it, and the menu says that the microphone isn't being recorded and how to allow it: in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone, then start a new recording. If there is no microphone, or capture won't start with it, On Air starts capture again without it, so the picture and its sound are still recorded, and the menu says why. Either way the menu says so while recording and once the recording is saved, and the next recording asks again.
- **Hearing the microphone.** While recording with the microphone, capture listens for its sound: any sample louder than 120 dB below full scale, which is far below the hiss of a working microphone. If none has come 5 seconds after capture started, the menu says that the microphone isn't being heard, whether nothing or only silence came from it, and what to do: allow it if macOS asked about it, then stop and start again, or else check that it's plugged in and not muted. It is meant to catch a microphone that macOS doesn't let On Air hear, and one unplugged or muted before the recording starts, none of which has been tried by hand. A microphone that goes quiet after it was heard, such as one unplugged mid-call, isn't noticed. The note goes once the sound comes. If it never comes, the menu says so once the recording is saved. The dot itself doesn't change.
- **Pieces.** Picture and sound go to disk in pieces 4 seconds long, in On Air's spool, `Library/Application Support/On Air/spool` in the home folder. A piece is the recording's opening segment followed by one fragment of fragmented MPEG-4, so each plays on its own, and it gets its name only once its bytes are on disk. When the screen is still, the last frame is written again each second, so pieces keep closing.
- **One timeline from zero.** A recording starts at time zero, in the joined file and in every piece, and its sound is on the same timeline as its picture. The file's own decode times put the first frame 26/600 s in, after the 2112 samples (44 ms) of priming the AAC encoder puts before the first sound, and AVFoundation reports both tracks from zero.
- **Stop.** Stop closes the last piece. The dot is grey again as soon as capture has stopped, and the pieces are joined into one file in the background, so Stop doesn't wait for the join however long the recording ran. The file is joined into the recording's own directory in the spool, under the name it will have in the folder, and then handed over, as below. If the join fails, the menu says where the pieces are, and its Show item opens their folder. The pieces stay, and the next launch tries the join again, as it does after a kill, and marks the file "(recovered)". Nothing is handed over until a join works. Once a recording is saved, the menu also says if it ended early because a piece couldn't be written, and what capture says it left out: frames or sound buffers the writer dropped, and sound that came too late or too early to mix.
- **Where recordings go.** To the folder a person picks under "Hand Recordings To" in the menu, with the system's folder picker, folders only: the one On the Record watches. The menu names the folder. Until one is picked, recordings go to a folder of On Air's own, `Movies/On Air` in the home folder, by the same steps, and the menu says so. The choice is remembered in `settings.json` with the others, and can be made at any time: a recording goes to the folder picked when its hand-over starts. A folder in On Air's spool is refused, and the menu says why. Recordings are named by the date and time they started, and "Open Recordings Folder" opens the folder they go to.
- **The hand-over.** [`internal/delivery`](internal/delivery) carries out each step the [proved core](#the-hand-over-core) decides. The recording is copied into the folder under a hidden name, its own name with a dot in front, which On the Record ignores, and flushed to disk. The copy is always a new file, so whatever was under the hidden name, a link included, is never written through. The copy is compared with the spool's file byte for byte, and only when the two match does it take the recording's name, through a hard link that never replaces a file, after which the hidden name goes. A copy that differs is removed and made again, and after three in a row the hand-over fails. A file already under the recording's name is compared with the spool's: the same bytes count as handed over, and anything else is left exactly as it is and fails the hand-over with a name conflict. On Air's own copy never counts as the folder's, as it would in a folder that leads into the spool. When a file the same as the recording took its name while the copy was made, the copy is removed from its hidden name, even after a kill. In the folder On Air asks for nothing but those two names.
- **Kept before it is acted on.** The core's record, and each step's result, are kept in `handover.json` in the recording's directory, written whole under a hidden name and renamed, before On Air acts on either. A hand-over cut short by a kill or a quit carries on from where it stood at the next launch, and a step asked for again gets the result it first had: a copy that was given its name before the record of that was kept is taken as the hand-over's own, and isn't copied again.
- **What the menu says.** While a hand-over runs, the menu says where the recording is going, and then where it went, and Show opens it in the folder. One that fails, because the folder is gone or its disk full, the name is taken, or its copies keep differing, keeps the spool's file: the menu says why and where the recording is, and Show opens it there. A failed hand-over is tried again at the next launch, and whenever a folder is picked. One that stops at a step On Air can't carry out, such as a hidden name another program holds as a folder, says what stopped it, and carries on from that step at those same times. One stuck where the core's model doesn't go, as below, says the recording is safe in the spool and stays there, since On Air can't carry it on by itself.
- **Three days.** On Air's own copy, the joined file in the spool, is removed three days after the folder was found to hold the recording, however long the copy took, at the next launch after that or within the hour while On Air runs. The days are on the Mac's clock: one set back keeps the copy longer, and one that was behind at the hand-over, or is set forward, has it go sooner. A removal that fails is tried again, and never undoes the hand-over. The pieces stay in the spool: when they may go is for the pieces' core, [#9](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/9). A recording joined before the hand-over existed is left as it is, in On Air's own folder.
- **After a kill.** If On Air is killed mid-recording, the pieces written so far stay on disk. At the next launch it joins the whole ones into a file marked "(recovered)", says how much it saved, and hands the file over like any other. It says so for each recording it finds, and for one it can't join, where its pieces are. The recording isn't carried on.
- **One at a time.** A second Start while a recording runs is refused, and says so. Quit while recording or starting finishes the recording first, hands it over, and then quits. Hand-overs run one at a time, in the background, so a new recording can start while the last one is handed over.

How it's built, in two layers, of which only the core decides:

| Part | What it does |
| :-- | :-- |
| `internal/recorder` | Decides: whether Live Context is on, what a recording captures and what it falls back to, one recording at a time, asking for the microphone before capture starts and going ahead without it when it is refused, when capture starts and stops, starting again without the microphone, what the menu says, including when the microphone isn't heard and how long capture waits for it, joining at Stop off the menu's thread, recovering what a kill left, where recordings go, and when each hand-over runs: after a join, at a launch, when a folder is picked, and hourly for On Air's own copies. It reaches the machine through an `Edge` it is given. |
| `internal/delivery` | Carries out the hand-over's steps on the real folder and the spool, as [the core](#the-hand-over-core) decides them, and keeps the core's record and each step's result on disk before acting on either. It decides nothing of its own about the hand-over. Plain Go, tested on Linux against real folders. |
| `internal/settings` | Keeps what was picked, where recordings go, and whether Live Context is on, in a small JSON file, written whole under a hidden name and then renamed. |
| `internal/live` and `internal/archive` | [Live Context](#live-context-a-first-experiment)'s decisions, and its client for On the Record's read-only door. |
| `internal/spool` | Keeps a recording's pieces: names them, tells a whole piece from a partial one, joins the whole ones from the first up to the first one missing, and marks a recording done. A file already at the join's name is never replaced. |
| `internal/mac` | The edge, cgo and Objective-C, built only on macOS. `app.m` draws the menu-bar item. `choose.m` lists the connected displays through `NSScreen`, which needs no permission, names each by its UUID, which outlasts reconnecting it, draws the overlay for an area, keeps the window the system's picker hands back, and shows the system's folder picker, `NSOpenPanel`, for folders only. `capture.m` asks macOS whether On Air may use the microphone, which asks the person the first time, and reports the answer. It takes the picture, the sound and the microphone from ScreenCaptureKit, on one serial queue: the display it is asked for, an area of it through `sourceRect`, or the picked window, and it reports what it can't find. It counts the microphone's buffers, and says once when none has held sound in the time Go gives it, and again if sound comes after that. `mixer.m` puts the two sounds on one 48 kHz stereo timeline and adds them. `writer.m` cuts picture and sound into segments with AVAssetWriter and hands each to Go as soon as it is whole. `selftest.m` drives the writer and the mixer with made-up frames and sound. `live.m` and `panel.m` are [Live Context](#live-context-a-first-experiment)'s speech recognition and panel. It reports what happened and decides nothing. |
| `internal/mp4` | Reads, from a fragmented MPEG-4 file's own bytes, where each track's first fragment starts. |
| `internal/tone` | Measures how loud a tone is in decoded sound, for the self-test. |
| `cmd/onair` | Wires the recorder to the edge, runs the hourly removal of On Air's own copies whose days are up, and runs the self-test. |
| `tools/build-app` | Builds and signs `On Air.app`, the bundle macOS gives the screen-recording and microphone permissions to. |

The mixer waits for whichever source is behind, half a second at most, then carries on without it, and advances with the clock when neither sends anything, so the sound track has no hole. A source's buffers are laid end to end, and its stamps only place the first one and any that strays by more than 20 ms. What the writer isn't ready for waits in order, up to four seconds, because a sound buffer left out closes up in the track and everything after it plays early. What is left out all the same is counted, and the menu says so once the recording is saved.

- **On a Mac, with no permission.** `onair selftest` writes ten seconds of made-up frames and sound through the same mixer and writer capture uses, stamped by the host clock as capture's are and given in the order capture would get them. Its writer isn't in real time: it waits for each input to be ready, where capture's writer holds sound back or drops a frame, so that part is only checked by a person. The display's sound comes as 48 kHz stereo planes carrying 440 Hz, louder on the left. The microphone's comes as 44.1 kHz mono carrying 880 Hz, a few milliseconds off the display's, with a second where it sends nothing and a switch to 48 kHz later on. The self-test checks that the mixer left nothing out, that no piece came after the writer finished, that a piece plays on its own, and that the joined file plays for the ten seconds written with one picture and one sound track. It checks that the file starts at zero, as AVFoundation reads it and as its own bytes say, and it decodes the sound and measures it in 50 ms blocks: both tones in both channels at the loudness they were made, the microphone's silent for its quiet second, with both edges within 50 ms of where they were made, and nothing at 660 Hz, which nothing played. `onair selftest 1` sends one sound track straight to the writer, and `onair selftest 2` shows the writer refusing a second one.

The same goes for the display list, the area overlay, the cropped picture, the window picker, what is recorded of a window, its sound included, the folder picker, and the hand-over to On the Record's real folder. The comparison reads both files through macOS's file cache, so it checks what the file system holds, not the disk's own blocks.
- **Checked by hand on the owner's Mac, on 14 November 2026,** with the build from `main` of that day, and reported working:

  - Stop, and playing the recording back.
  - A hard kill: On Air was killed with `SIGKILL` 33 seconds into a recording. At the next launch it joined the 7 whole pieces into a recovered file of 28.6 seconds that starts at time 0, losing only the piece that was open.
  - Recording a single window while another window covered it.
  - Recording an area of a display.
- **Checked by hand later the same day,** with [#7](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/7)'s change: macOS asked for the microphone before the first recording started, and that recording had the voice.
- **Not yet checked by hand:** a 60-minute call, for the picture and sound staying in step over time and the file's size; a microphone unplugged mid-call; the Mac going to sleep, and logging out, mid-recording; the picked display unplugged; and a microphone whose clock drifts from the display's, the known weakness of the mixer's realignment. Nor has going ahead when the microphone is refused, or the note when its sound doesn't come. Nor has the hand-over, which came later: picking On the Record's folder, recording a minute and finding it there under its name, killing On Air during the hand-over of a long recording and finding one whole file after the relaunch, and a different file under the next recording's name left as it was, with the menu saying so ([#11](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/11)). None of the milestone's hand checks in [`docs/PRD.md`](docs/PRD.md#trying-milestone-one-by-hand) has been done in full.

### What it doesn't do yet

- Hand a recording over when the folder's disk can't give a file a second name, as exFAT and some network shares can't: the copy takes its name through a hard link, so it never replaces a file. The hand-over stops, and the menu says why. Like any step On Air can't carry out, it is carried on in that folder, even once another is picked, since an attempt keeps to its folder until it ends.
- Get past two places the core's model doesn't go. A file that takes the recording's name while the copy is made, and is gone again by the time it is compared, leaves the core asking for the same comparison, and a copy removed from under its hidden name before it is given its name leaves it nothing to give. Either way the hand-over stops and keeps the spool's file, and the menu says On Air can't carry it on by itself. It looks again at each launch and folder pick, and stops the same way, whatever folder is picked. [#16](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/16) asks for the core to end the attempt in both, so that it is tried again as a failed one is.
- Try a failed hand-over again by itself while On Air runs. That happens at a launch, and when a folder is picked.
- Keep a second On Air away from the first one's recordings. Two copies of the app running at once share the spool, and nothing stops both handing over the same recording.
- Guard the disk (rule 8), whose core is [#10](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/10), or the length of a recording (decision 4).
- Try a failed join again when the person asks, or leave out a piece the join can't read (rule 9). Today such a piece fails the whole join.
- Carry a recording on after On Air or its recorder dies (decision 3). A killed recording is recovered as it stands at the next launch.
- Start capture again when it stops mid-recording, for the microphone or anything else. The recording ends there and what was recorded is kept.
- Record more than one display or window at once, or change what is recorded while a recording runs.
- Stop by itself, or anything in milestones two and three beyond [Live Context](#live-context-a-first-experiment)'s first experiment.

## Live Context, a first experiment

[#18](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/18) builds a first, experimental piece of milestone three ahead of the milestones before it: while On Air records, a panel beside the call shows what the last minute of the conversation connects to in On the Record. It has no decision model yet, so it shows On the Record's own strongest matches, not judgements about them. It was built by hand and hasn't been tried on a Mac: [what that leaves unchecked](#how-live-context-is-checked) is below.

- **On and off.** "Show Live Context" in the menu turns it on or off at any time, recording or not, and the choice is remembered in `settings.json`. It is off until it is turned on. Turning it on asks macOS whether On Air may recognize speech, which macOS asks the person the first time, so the question comes before the first recording that uses it. If it still hasn't been asked when a recording starts, it is asked then, and the recording doesn't wait for the answer.
- **Listening.** While a recording runs with Live Context on, the Mac recognizes the speech in capture's two sounds, which it receives apart before they are mixed: the microphone's words are taken as the person's, and the call's sound as everyone else's. Recognition runs on the Mac only (`requiresOnDeviceRecognition`). If the Mac can't recognize the language by itself, nothing is recognized and the panel says so: the call's words are never sent to Apple. Only finished text is kept. A request ends every 15 seconds, so each sound's words come that often, and a word said across an end can be lost. If recognition is refused, the panel says how to allow it, and the recording goes on without it.
- **What capture gives it.** A copy of each sound buffer, made on capture's queue, which does nothing else for it and never waits for it. When 512 copies wait for recognition, the next ones are dropped, and the panel says that it fell behind.
- **The last minute.** The finished text whose end is less than a minute old, 4,000 characters at most, the newest kept.
- **Asking On the Record.** At most every 15 seconds, only when something new was said and the minute holds 8 words or more, On Air sends the minute's text, in the order it was said, to `find_connections` at On the Record's read-only door on this Mac, `http://127.0.0.1:8797/mcp/readonly`, asking for 5 of each kind. A question that has no answer in 4 seconds is given up on, and while two given up on are still out, no other is asked. `internal/archive` takes only a loopback IP address, dials nothing else, uses no proxy, follows no redirect, reads 4 MB of an answer at most, and calls no tool but `find_connections`. Nothing is written to On the Record.
- **Which links.** From each answer, the earlier meetings and the open or on-hold action items that On the Record itself suggests linking, which are those nearly as strong as its best match, and the first notebook entry it matched by meaning, which it does only past its own threshold. The two strongest meetings come first, then an open item, then the notebook entry, then any other meeting. A link shown in the last five minutes isn't shown again. New links go on top, and the panel keeps three. Each says what it leads to and its date, its title, and a line of why: for a meeting, the moment that matched and when it was said in that meeting; for an item, whether it is open or on hold, whose it is and the meeting it came from; for a notebook entry, that it is close in meaning and the words it shares. A click opens it in the browser, http or https only.
- **The panel.** A slim panel, 300 points wide, at the top right of the display being recorded, or of the main display for a window, which floats above other windows and over full-screen apps and never takes focus. It shows a status line: how many words were heard in the last minute and how many of them were the person's, that an answer brought nothing new, or what went wrong. It shows while recording with Live Context on, and stays two minutes after the recording stops when it shows links. It is marked `NSWindowSharingNone` to keep it out of other apps' captures, such as a call's screen share. While Live Context is on, On Air's own capture of a display leaves On Air itself out of the picture, so the panel isn't recorded, nor is the menu-bar dot; a window's recording never holds it anyway. A recording that started before Live Context was turned on doesn't leave it out, and the panel says so.
- **Apart from the recording.** The live side runs on goroutines and queues of its own. It hears what the recorder does through a tap on the recorder's edge, which passes each call on unchanged before the live side hears of it, and every input it takes returns at once: a field set, or a bounded queue of finished text and reports whose oldest go when it is full. A panic in it is recovered, and after three it stops, and the panel says so. What goes wrong on the live side is said only in the panel, never in the menu.

| Part | What it does |
| :-- | :-- |
| `internal/live` | Decides: the last minute of what was said and whose it was, when to ask On the Record, which links to show and for how long, and what the panel says. Its `Controller` runs it on goroutines of its own, and `Tap` lets it hear what the recorder does. Plain Go, with no network code, tested on Linux. |
| `internal/archive` | Asks On the Record's read-only door: MCP, JSON-RPC over HTTP, answered as JSON or an event stream, to a loopback address only. It decides nothing. It is the one part of On Air that speaks over a network. |
| `internal/mac` | `live.m` recognizes speech in copies of capture's two sounds, at 16 kHz in one channel, on a queue of its own, and reports finished words with when they were said. `panel.m` draws the panel. `capture.m` hands over the copies and leaves On Air out of a display's picture while Live Context is on. `app.m` has the menu item. |

### How Live Context is checked

- **CI, on Linux.** Tests of the last minute (what it keeps, times that can't be right, both speakers in the order they spoke), of when to ask, of which links are picked and what each says, of links that can't be opened, of new links on top and none repeated within five minutes, of a late or abandoned answer, and of what the panel shows and says in each case. The controller is tested against a fake On the Record and a fake Mac: an answer shown, nothing asked when nothing new was said, an archive that is down, slow, never answers, panics or has no address, a panel that panics or hangs, and inputs that return at once and stay bounded whatever it does. The client is tested against a fake read-only door, in the shapes learned from the door's `tools/list` and from On the Record's code: the answer read, the session started once and again when forgotten, JSON and event-stream answers, every error it can give and in good time, a door that isn't there, loopback only, no proxy and no redirect. The recorder's tests cover the setting. And one test records the same meeting without the live side and then with it failing in each of those ways, and finds every call the recorder made of the Mac, in order, and every file it left, the same each time.
- **On a Mac, with no permission,** `go test ./...` builds the Objective-C, and the self-test passes as before.
- **Not yet tried on a Mac:** speech recognition at all, its permission and its two sounds, what the Mac reports and how fast; the panel's place, look in light and dark mode, and clicks; that the panel is out of the recording and out of a call's screen share; and the whole loop with On the Record's server running. Whether a screen share leaves the panel out depends on how the call's app captures: macOS may not keep a window marked `NSWindowSharingNone` out of every capture.

### What Live Context doesn't do yet

- The decision model and its typed questions, the brainstorm and review profiles, decisions that drift from earlier meetings, speaker names, and the draft decision list (decisions 9 and 10).
- Show a count on the menu-bar dot when the panel is closed (decision 11), or look every 10 seconds and when the screen changes (decision 12): it asks every 15 seconds, and listens only.
- Read the open items of the people on a call at its start, or use `search`.
- Close the panel by itself, other than by turning Live Context off.

## The hand-over core

[`core/handover`](core/handover) decides each step of handing a finished recording to the folder another program watches: copy it there under a hidden name, compare the copy with the original, give it its real name without replacing anything, and remove the original once its time to keep it has passed. It touches no file itself. Its caller carries out each step and reports what happened, so a hand-over can be interrupted at any step and carried on from its record. It is its own Go module.

Invariant's factory built it from [#1](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/1), and it merged through [#3](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/pull/3):

- 42 statements, ratified on the issue and pinned by hash in [`ratified.lock`](core/handover/.invariant/ratified.lock): 18 invariants, 13 witnesses, 5 known bugs, 4 fairness conditions, 1 liveness property and the spec.
- A TLA+ model, [`RecordingHandover.tla`](core/handover/.invariant/specs/RecordingHandover.tla), which TLC explores exhaustively within `MaxAttempts = 2`, `MaxCleanupAttempts = 2` and `MaxDifferences = 2`: 7,296 distinct states to depth 60, no violation and no deadlock, all 13 witnesses reached, the liveness property holding, and all 5 known bugs caught. One size larger, the code reaches the model's states again: 32,955 of them.
- Go code that Gobra proves against its contracts (10 of 10 functions), and that is tested against the model: 247,592 steps recorded, all 7,296 model states visited.
- A second review of its explorer, the code that walks the model's states, left two notes on the pull request, which aren't taken up yet: `MaxDifferences` is missing from the manifest's parameters, and a refused step's returned record isn't observed.

The gate verifies it again on every pull request. The app reaches it as the owner chose in [#11](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/11): it requires the core's tagged release, `github.com/gitdek/onair/core/handover v0.1.0`, pinned by hash in `go.sum`. The tag `core/handover/v0.1.0` is on `dec76f5`, where the gate had verified the core unchanged since [#3](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/pull/3) merged it, so the app builds against the code that was verified. A change to the core needs a new tag, and the app moves to it in a change of its own. [`internal/delivery`](internal/delivery) is its caller, so rules 5, 6, 7 and 11 are held by the app through it: proved in the core for its decisions, and tested on real folders for the steps.

## The repository

- Its checks, in `.invariant/repository.json`: formatting, vet, build and tests, run in a pinned Go image. It declares `go.sum` as the app's lockfile, which the gate needs before a pull request may require a Go module, and reads from the base branch, never from the pull request.
- Its gate, `.github/workflows/gate.yml`, which runs those checks and verifies each modeled project on every pull request.
- The README's graphics, drawn by `docs/assets/generate.py` in a light and a dark theme.
