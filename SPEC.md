# On Air: what is built

This page describes On Air as it is. [`docs/PRD.md`](docs/PRD.md) describes what it is meant to become.

Milestone one is under way. Two parts exist: a menu-bar recorder that keeps each recording in pieces on disk, and the proved core of the hand-over, through which the recorder hands each finished recording to the folder picked from its menu. The proved cores of a recording's life and of its pieces are merged too, and nothing calls them yet. Beside them is a first, experimental piece of milestone three, [Live Context](#live-context-a-first-experiment), tried by hand on the owner's Mac, in a real meeting among other tries. The recorder was checked by hand on the owner's Mac on 14 November 2026, and [what was checked and what wasn't](#how-its-checked) is below. None of the milestone's own hand checks in [`docs/PRD.md`](docs/PRD.md#trying-milestone-one-by-hand) has been done in full.

## The recorder

What it does:

- **The menu bar.** A dot, grey when idle and red with a running clock while recording. Its menu has Start or Stop, a note when there is something to say, what to record, where recordings go, Show Live Context, the last recording, the recordings folder and Quit. There is no keyboard shortcut yet.
- **What it records.** What a person picks under Record in the menu: one of the connected displays, listed by name and size, an area of the picked display, or a single window. Until they pick, the whole main display. The picture keeps the shape of what it records, brought down to at most 1080 rows with even sides, at most 15 frames a second, in H.264 at 1.8 Mb/s, with the pointer. The sound and the microphone are mixed into one AAC track, 48 kHz stereo at 128 kb/s. On Air's own sound is left out.
- **Picking.** Picking a display, or "Whole Display", takes effect at the next Start. "Select Area…" dims the picked display and lets a person drag out a rectangle, at least 64 points on each side: Return or letting go of the mouse picks it, and Escape leaves the choice as it was. A line on the dimmed display says so: "Drag to select · Return or let go to confirm · Esc to cancel", near its top, or near its bottom when the rectangle is in the way. "Select Window…" opens the system's window picker, limited to one window. A display or an area is remembered between recordings and launches, in `settings.json` beside the spool, written whole under a hidden name and then renamed. A window lasts until it closes or On Air quits. Nothing can be picked while a recording runs.
- **When the pick isn't there.** If the picked display isn't connected at Start, On Air records the main display; if the area no longer fits its display, the whole display; if the picked window is gone, the whole display. The menu says so while recording and once the recording is saved, and a remembered choice never keeps a recording from starting. A window that closes while it is recorded ends the recording, as capture stopping by itself does: what was recorded is kept, and the menu says why. ScreenCaptureKit says that a window closed from macOS 15.2 on; on earlier versions On Air notices when its menu is next opened.
- **A window's picture and sound.** The picture is the window alone, whatever covers it. Its size is set when the recording starts, from the size the picker's filter gives for the window, which may be the size it had when it was picked. If it is resized, ScreenCaptureKit is asked to scale it to fit that size, keeping its shape. Apple's WWDC22 session "Take ScreenCaptureKit to the next level" says that a single-window filter captures all the sound of the application that owns the window, from its other windows too, and no other application's. The SDK's headers don't say. Recording a window while another covered it was checked by hand. A resized window, and which application's sound a window's recording holds, haven't been tried.
- **Asking for the microphone.** Start asks for the microphone before capture starts. When macOS hasn't asked the person yet, it asks them then, the menu says the recording starts once that question is answered, and it starts when it is. Otherwise macOS answers at once. If the microphone is refused, the recording goes ahead without it, and the menu says that the microphone isn't being recorded and how to allow it: in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone, then start a new recording. If there is no microphone, or capture won't start with it, On Air starts capture again without it, so the picture and its sound are still recorded, and the menu says why. Either way the menu says so while recording and once the recording is saved, and the next recording asks again.
- **Hearing the microphone.** While recording with the microphone, capture listens for its sound: any sample louder than 120 dB below full scale, which is far below the hiss of a working microphone. If none has come 5 seconds after capture started, the menu says that the microphone isn't being heard, whether nothing or only silence came from it, and what to do: allow it if macOS asked about it, then stop and start again, or else check that it's plugged in and not muted. It is meant to catch a microphone that macOS doesn't let On Air hear, and one unplugged or muted before the recording starts, none of which has been tried by hand. A microphone that goes quiet after it was heard, such as one unplugged mid-call, isn't noticed. The note goes once the sound comes. If it never comes, the menu says so once the recording is saved. The dot itself doesn't change.
- **Pieces.** Picture and sound go to disk in pieces 4 seconds long, in On Air's spool, `Library/Application Support/On Air/spool` in the home folder. A piece is the recording's opening segment followed by one fragment of fragmented MPEG-4, so each plays on its own, and it gets its name only once its bytes are on disk. When the screen is still, the last frame is written again each second, so pieces keep closing.
- **One timeline from zero.** A recording starts at time zero, in the joined file and in every piece, and its sound is on the same timeline as its picture. The file's own decode times put the first frame 26/600 s in, after the 2112 samples (44 ms) of priming the AAC encoder puts before the first sound, and AVFoundation reports both tracks from zero.
- **Stop.** Stop closes the last piece. The dot is grey again as soon as capture has stopped, and the pieces are joined into one file in the background, so Stop doesn't wait for the join however long the recording ran. The file is joined into the recording's own directory in the spool, under the name it will have in the folder, and then handed over, as below. If the join fails, the menu says where the pieces are, and its Show item opens their folder. The pieces stay, and the next launch tries the join again, as it does after a kill, and marks the file "(recovered)". Nothing is handed over until a join works. Once a recording is saved, the menu also says if it ended early because a piece couldn't be written, and what capture says it left out: frames or sound buffers the writer dropped, and sound that came too late or too early to mix.
- **Where recordings go.** To the folder a person picks under "Hand Recordings To" in the menu, with the system's folder picker, folders only: the one On the Record watches. The menu names the folder. Until one is picked, recordings go to a folder of On Air's own, `Movies/On Air` in the home folder, by the same steps, and the menu says so. The choice is remembered in `settings.json` with the others, and can be made at any time: a recording goes to the folder picked when its hand-over starts. A folder in On Air's spool is refused, and the menu says why. Recordings are named by the date and time they started, and "Open Recordings Folder" opens the folder they go to.
- **The hand-over.** [`internal/delivery`](internal/delivery) carries out each step the [proved core](#the-hand-over-core) decides. The recording is copied into the folder under a hidden name, its own name with a dot in front, which On the Record ignores, and flushed to disk. The copy is always a new file, so whatever was under the hidden name, a link included, is never written through. The copy is compared with the spool's file byte for byte, and only when the two match does it take the recording's name, through a hard link that never replaces a file, after which the hidden name goes. A copy that differs is removed and made again, and after three in a row the hand-over fails. A file already under the recording's name is compared with the spool's: the same bytes count as handed over, and anything else is left exactly as it is and fails the hand-over with a name conflict. On Air's own copy never counts as the folder's, as it would in a folder that leads into the spool. When a file the same as the recording took its name while the copy was made, the copy is removed from its hidden name, even after a kill. In the folder On Air asks for nothing but those two names.
- **Kept before it is acted on.** The core's record, and each step's result, are kept in `handover.json` in the recording's directory, written whole under a hidden name and renamed, before On Air acts on either. A hand-over cut short by a kill or a quit carries on from where it stood at the next launch, and a step asked for again gets the result it first had: a copy that was given its name before the record of that was kept is taken as the hand-over's own, and isn't copied again.
- **What the menu says.** While a hand-over runs, the menu says where the recording is going, and then where it went, and Show opens it in the folder. One that fails, because the folder is gone or its disk full, the name is taken, or its copies keep differing, keeps the spool's file: the menu says why and where the recording is, and Show opens it there. A failed hand-over is tried again at the next launch, and whenever a folder is picked. One that stops at a step On Air can't carry out, such as a hidden name another program holds as a folder, says what stopped it, and carries on from that step at those same times. One stuck where the core's model doesn't go, as below, says the recording is safe in the spool and stays there, since On Air can't carry it on by itself.
- **Three days.** On Air's own copy, the joined file in the spool, is removed three days after the folder was found to hold the recording, however long the copy took, at the next launch after that or within the hour while On Air runs. The days are on the Mac's clock: one set back keeps the copy longer, and one that was behind at the hand-over, or is set forward, has it go sooner. A removal that fails is tried again, and never undoes the hand-over. The pieces stay in the spool: when they may go is for the pieces' core, [#9](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/9). A recording joined before the hand-over existed is left as it is, in On Air's own folder.
- **After a kill.** If On Air is killed mid-recording, the pieces written so far stay on disk. At the next launch it joins the whole ones into a file marked "(recovered)", says how much it saved, and hands the file over like any other. It says so for each recording it finds, and for one it can't join, where its pieces are. The recording isn't carried on.
- **One at a time.** A second Start while a recording runs is refused, and says so. Quit while recording or starting finishes the recording first, hands it over, and then quits. Hand-overs run one at a time, in the background, so a new recording can start while the last one is handed over.

How it's built, in two layers, of which only the core decides:

| Part | What it does |
| :-- | :-- |
| `internal/recorder` | Decides: whether Live Context is on, what a recording captures and what it falls back to, one recording at a time, asking for the microphone before capture starts and going ahead without it when it is refused, when capture starts and stops, starting again without the microphone, what the menu says, including when the microphone isn't heard and how long capture waits for it, joining at Stop off the menu's thread, recovering what a kill left, where recordings go, and when each hand-over runs: after a join, at a launch, when a folder is picked, and hourly for On Air's own copies. It reaches the machine through an `Edge` it is given. |
| `internal/delivery` | Carries out the hand-over's steps on the real folder and the spool, as [the core](#the-hand-over-core) decides them, and keeps the core's record and each step's result on disk before acting on either. It decides nothing of its own about the hand-over. Plain Go, tested on Linux against real folders. |
| `internal/settings` | Keeps what was picked, where recordings go, and whether Live Context is on, in a small JSON file, written whole under a hidden name and then renamed. |
| `internal/live` and `internal/archive` | [Live Context](#live-context-a-first-experiment)'s decisions, and its client for On the Record's read-only door. |
| `internal/spool` | Keeps a recording's pieces: names them, tells a whole piece from a partial one, joins the whole ones from the first up to the first one missing, and marks a recording done. A file already at the join's name is never replaced. |
| `internal/mac` | The edge, cgo and Objective-C, built only on macOS. `app.m` draws the menu-bar item. `choose.m` lists the connected displays through `NSScreen`, which needs no permission, names each by its UUID, which outlasts reconnecting it, draws the overlay for an area, keeps the window the system's picker hands back, and shows the system's folder picker, `NSOpenPanel`, for folders only. `capture.m` asks macOS whether On Air may use the microphone, which asks the person the first time, and reports the answer. It takes the picture, the sound and the microphone from ScreenCaptureKit, on one serial queue: the display it is asked for, an area of it through `sourceRect`, or the picked window, and it reports what it can't find. It counts the microphone's buffers, and says once when none has held sound in the time Go gives it, and again if sound comes after that. `mixer.m` puts the two sounds on one 48 kHz stereo timeline and adds them. `writer.m` cuts picture and sound into segments with AVAssetWriter and hands each to Go as soon as it is whole. `selftest.m` drives the writer and the mixer with made-up frames and sound. `live.m` and `panel.m` are [Live Context](#live-context-a-first-experiment)'s speech recognition and panel. It reports what happened and decides nothing. |
| `internal/mp4` | Reads, from a fragmented MPEG-4 file's own bytes, where each track's first fragment starts. |
| `internal/tone` | Measures how loud a tone is in decoded sound, for the self-test. |
| `cmd/onair` | Wires the recorder to the edge, runs the hourly removal of On Air's own copies whose days are up, and runs the self-test. |
| `tools/build-app` | Builds and signs `On Air.app`, the bundle macOS gives the screen-recording and microphone permissions to. |

The mixer waits for whichever source is behind, half a second at most, then carries on without it, and advances with the clock when neither sends anything, so the sound track has no hole. A source's buffers are laid end to end, and its stamps only place the first one and any that strays by more than 20 ms. What the writer isn't ready for waits in order, up to four seconds, because a sound buffer left out closes up in the track and everything after it plays early. What is left out all the same is counted, and the menu says so once the recording is saved.

- **On a Mac, with no permission.** `onair selftest` writes ten seconds of made-up frames and sound through the same mixer and writer capture uses, stamped by the host clock as capture's are and given in the order capture would get them. The display's sound comes as 48 kHz stereo planes carrying 440 Hz, louder on the left. The microphone's comes as 44.1 kHz mono carrying 880 Hz, a few milliseconds off the display's, with a second where it sends nothing and a switch to 48 kHz later on. The self-test checks that the mixer left nothing out, that no piece came after the writer finished, that a piece plays on its own, and that the joined file plays for the ten seconds written with one picture and one sound track. It checks that the file starts at zero, as AVFoundation reads it and as its own bytes say, and it decodes the sound and measures it in 50 ms blocks: both tones in both channels at the loudness they were made, the microphone's silent for its quiet second, with both edges within 50 ms of where they were made, and nothing at 660 Hz, which nothing played. `onair selftest 1` sends one sound track straight to the writer, and `onair selftest 2` shows the writer refusing a second one.

The same goes for the display list, the area overlay, the cropped picture, the window picker, what is recorded of a window, its sound included, the folder picker, and the hand-over to On the Record's real folder. The comparison reads both files through macOS's file cache, so it checks what the file system holds, not the disk's own blocks.

### What it doesn't do yet

- Hand a recording over when the folder's disk can't give a file a second name, as exFAT and some network shares can't: the copy takes its name through a hard link, so it never replaces a file. The hand-over stops, and the menu says why. Like any step On Air can't carry out, it is carried on in that folder, even once another is picked, since an attempt keeps to its folder until it ends.
- Get past two places the core's model doesn't go. A file that takes the recording's name while the copy is made, and is gone again by the time it is compared, leaves the core asking for the same comparison, and a copy removed from under its hidden name before it is given its name leaves it nothing to give. Either way the hand-over stops and keeps the spool's file, and the menu says On Air can't carry it on by itself. It looks again at each launch and folder pick, and stops the same way, whatever folder is picked. [#16](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/16) asks for the core to end the attempt in both, so that it is tried again as a failed one is.
- Try a failed hand-over again by itself while On Air runs. That happens at a launch, and when a folder is picked.
- Keep a second On Air away from the first one's recordings. Two copies of the app running at once share the spool, and nothing stops both handing over the same recording.
- Guard the disk (rule 8), whose core is [#10](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/10), or the length of a recording (decision 4).
- Try a failed join again when the person asks, or leave out a piece the join can't read (rule 9). Today such a piece fails the whole join.
- Carry a recording on after On Air or its recorder dies (decision 3). A killed recording is recovered as it stands at the next launch.
- Start capture again when it stops mid-recording, for the microphone or anything else. The recording ends there and what was recorded is kept.
- Record more than one display or window at once, or change what is recorded while a recording runs.
- Stop by itself, or anything in milestones two and three beyond [Live Context](#live-context-a-first-experiment)'s first experiment.

## Live Context, a first experiment

[#18](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/18) builds a first, experimental piece of milestone three ahead of the milestones before it: while On Air records, a panel beside the call shows what the conversation brings up that On the Record knows: a card about a project, a person or a company it knows by name as soon as the name is said, the open items of the people on the call, and the answer to a question about something it knows. Without its local models, which are optional, a card shows what the archive's own lists hold, and an answer the lines of its notes that share a question's words, not judgements about them. It was built by hand, tried by hand on the owner's Mac, in a real meeting among other tries, and tuned since on a replay of one of the owner's own calls: [what was checked and what wasn't](#how-live-context-is-checked) is below.

- **On and off.** "Show Live Context" in the menu turns it on or off at any time, recording or not, and the choice is remembered in `settings.json`. It is off until it is turned on. Turning it on asks macOS whether On Air may recognize speech, which macOS asks the person the first time, so the question comes before the first recording that uses it. If it still hasn't been asked when a recording starts, it is asked then, and the recording doesn't wait for the answer.
- **Listening.** While a recording runs with Live Context on, the Mac recognizes the speech in capture's two sounds, which it receives apart before they are mixed. Only one recognition runs at a time in a process: a first try on a Mac, with a recognizer for each sound, found that the microphone's requests ended at once, reporting no speech, while the call's ran. So the live side adds the two into one sound of its own, in one channel at the rate recognition asks for, placed by their timestamps as the recording's mixer places them, and one recognizer hears it. Before adding them, it measures how loud each was in every tenth of a second, and a word of a finished result is the person's when the microphone was more than twice as loud as the call's sound while it was said, and everyone else's otherwise, as when both talk at once; when a result gives its words no times, all of it goes by its whole sound. The factor is a first guess. Recognition runs on the Mac only (`requiresOnDeviceRecognition`), and is asked to add punctuation (`addsPunctuation`), whose full stops and question marks help tell where a sentence ends and whether it asks something. If the Mac can't recognize the language by itself, nothing is recognized and the panel says so: the call's words are never sent to Apple. Recognition reports partial results as it hears (`shouldReportPartialResults`), each holding all of its request's words so far, which later ones may revise, and a final result when the request ends. Each partial result goes to the live side when its words change, and four times a second the live side is told how far the request's sound has gone, so that it can tell a pause; only finished sentences are taken from them (see [answers](#answers)), and only final results go into the last minute. A request ends after 15 seconds of sound, and the next begins once it has finished, with its words or an error, or a second after it was called off for having given neither 5 seconds after its sound ended. The sound in between is held, 30 seconds at most, and goes to the next request, so none is skipped, though a word said across an end can be lost. A request that reports no speech, or is called off after its words came or its sound ended, has ended normally. Anything else recognition reports is said in the panel, a request that stops before its sound ended among them, and the next then waits a second. If recognition is refused, the panel says how to allow it, and the recording goes on without it. While recognition is being diagnosed, On Air keeps a log of the live side in its own folder, `live-diag.log`, moved aside to `live-diag.log.1` at 1 MB: what each sound and the mix of them were given and gave back, when each request began and how it ended, every error recognition reported, normal ends included, how many words of each final result were taken as whose and how loud each sound was, the words that reached the live side and its questions, for each request how many partial results came, with times or without, how far recognition was behind the sound, and how many sentences were finished as they were said and at the final, and how, the questions noticed in the call, how long after their last word, and those asked early, kept or dropped, the questions reworded and how many words were added, the answers asked for, how long each took and how many tokens their lines came to, and the lines shown, the local models' rounds, answers and checks, how long each took and what came of each, and for each card how long it took from its question's last word to its answer, step by step (answered, citing how many sources, not in the archive, rejected and why in On Air's own words, a difference shown or not), with running counts of each, the archive's lists, how long they took and how many meetings, items, entries and names they held, for each card about a name whether it is a person's or a project's or company's, heard in a finished sentence or one still being said, how many lines the lists gave it, how long On the Record and the writing model took, how many of the lines written were kept and left out and why the first was in On Air's own words, and how many questions named nothing the archive knows, in times, counts, levels, formats and codes only, and macOS's own words for an error of recognition, never words that were said, anything On the Record answered, or anything a model wrote.
- **What capture gives it.** A copy of each sound buffer, made on capture's queue, which does nothing else for it and never waits for it. When 512 copies wait for recognition, the next ones are dropped, and the panel says that it fell behind.
- **The last minute.** The finished text whose end is less than a minute old, 4,000 characters at most, the newest kept.
- **Asking On the Record.** As listening starts, On Air asks On the Record's read-only door on this Mac, `http://127.0.0.1:8797/mcp/readonly`, for its lists: `list_meetings`, `list_action_items` for the open items, and `list_notes`, given up on after 10 seconds and asked again a minute after a failure. Of an item it reads what it says, whose it is, where it stands and the meeting it came from, and never the evidence or sources behind it, which may point at a person's mail. For [cards](#topic-cards) and [answers](#answers) it asks `gather_context`. It no longer asks `find_connections` what the last minute connects to: in a real call its links were rarely about what was being said, and `Every`, the least time between two such questions, is zero, which asks none; set, it asks as before, at most that often, only when something new was said and the minute holds 8 words or more, for 5 of each kind, given up on after 10 seconds, and no other while two given up on are still out. `internal/archive` takes only a loopback IP address, dials nothing else, uses no proxy, follows no redirect, reads 4 MB of an answer at most, and calls no tool but those five, which only read; it never asks `gather_context` for the screen's moments, since the archive's owners may have a hosted model match those, and asks only in plain words, never in the filters its search reads, such as `in:screen`. Nothing is written to On the Record.
- <a id="names"></a>**The names the archive knows.** From its lists, On Air learns the names a card can be about: the people who were in its meetings and those its items belong to; and the projects and companies that its meetings' and notebook entries' titles, its meetings' summaries and its items write with a capital where a plain word would have none: inside a word, as a made-up "BrightLeaf" does, in the middle of a sentence, or, in a title, with none of its words ever written without one. What a meeting's title calls the meeting, such as review or call, a month or a day, an acronym alone (two capitals or more and two small letters at most) and a person's name are no project's. A person named on at least half of the items on the owners' lane of the board is one of the archive's owners, who are on the call themselves: they get no card of their own. A name is heard when its words come together in what was said, as written, run together or split ("Bright Leaf"); for a name of five letters or more, also as it sounds ("Pria" for "Priya"); written with a capital where no sentence starts, as a name is, misheard by a letter in a name of six letters or more, or by two in one of eight or more; and a name of eight letters or more as two words with its first four and last three letters as written and a few wrong between. A name of four letters or fewer is taken as written, or with a capital as it sounds; one that is also an everyday word only with a capital. A first name two people share isn't taken alone. A sweep of a dictionary's words, and of the replayed call's, through the archive's real names, on the owner's Mac, set these: before a misheard name needed a capital, everyday words a letter or two from a name were taken for it. The letters each tolerance needs are `HearShort` and `HearLong` in `live.Defaults`.
- <a id="topic-cards"></a>**Cards about names.** When a name the archive knows is heard, in a finished sentence or in one still being said, its card goes on top at once, titled with the name, with up to three lines from the lists: its open items, newest first, whose they are and the first sentence of what they say; the newest meeting it was in or is named in, with the archive's line on it; and the newest notebook entry about it. With the local models chosen, On Air then asks `gather_context` about the name, one card at a time, newest first, for 12 passages of the notes, and the writing model writes the card from the brief's lines, including what it quotes of notebook entries, those that say the name first, and a figure, a date or a decision, and a meeting of the last two weeks, counting more, numbered, to about 1,200 tokens. It isn't given action items: the card keeps the open items the lists gave it, with whose they are, which the model was seen to get wrong. It is asked for three to five lines, each one fact of 12 words at most, the most useful in a conversation first (amounts, numbers and dates; what was decided; what is still open and who owns it; the next step), no background, who owns or did each thing as the notes say, a suggestion or a plan not taken for a decision, nor what a notebook entry advises for what happened, and the number of its note after each. Go keeps a line only when it cites only numbers it was given and nothing else in brackets, has three words or more and 120 characters at most, doesn't copy the form it was shown, and gives in digits only figures that a note it cites gives, as an answer must; four at most. The written lines replace the lists' once they come, after the card's open items, two at most, the card keeping its place, each line with its sources as small chips, the date of its meeting or entry, which a click opens. A line whose source isn't one of On the Record's own links, on the host its meetings' links are on, is never shown. A fact, by what it comes from, shown on another card in the last 10 minutes isn't shown again, and a card left with nothing to show isn't shown. A name said again while its card is on the panel, or within 10 minutes, brings no other card, but once two minutes have passed a finished sentence that says something else about it brings a card about that: On the Record is asked the name and those words, and the card, which replaces the name's card on the panel, shows only what wasn't shown lately, and nothing when that is nothing. The writer takes a card only when no question waits to be reworded, answered or written: a question asked comes first. A card's question to On the Record is given up on after 10 seconds, and its writing after 8 and a second. The lines of what On the Record gathered are kept to check what is said against, as an answer's are. The numbers are with the others in `live.Defaults`, and were set on a [replay](#how-live-context-is-checked) of the owner's own call.
- <a id="standing-card"></a>**The standing card.** Once the lists come, a card titled "Open items" shows the open items on the owners' lane of the board, newest first, four at most, each as whose it is, the people the archive knows its owner names, and the first sentence of what it says. When someone the archive knows is named in the first minute of listening, their open items join it, and it goes on top again. It needs no model, and its items aren't shown again on a card about a name for 10 minutes.
- <a id="answers"></a>**Answers.** A question asked in the call that names a project, a person or a company the archive knows, but its owners, or asks about the last time something happened, gets an answer card, from On the Record's notes, which on the owner's Mac showed its answer about 3 seconds after the question. In a conversation most questions are for the people in it, and one that names nothing the archive knows is left to them, and, since it asks something, isn't checked against the record as a statement either; with no names known, as when the archive's lists couldn't be had, every question is asked, as before. The words of each result of recognition not yet taken are split into sentences where recognition marks an end, with a full stop, a question mark or an exclamation mark, and where 0.7 seconds of silence falls between two words. Of a partial result, a sentence followed by more words is finished at once, and the last one said is finished once recognition has been given 0.4 seconds more sound with its end mark standing, or 0.7 and 0.4 seconds more after its last word; when a partial result gives its words no times, once 0.7 seconds of sound brought nothing new. The rest of a request is finished at its final result, or, when that never comes, as last heard once the next request begins. Each sentence is taken once, however later results revise it: words whose middle falls before the end of the last sentence taken, or, without times, as many words as were taken, are left out of the next. A sentence that runs to the end of a request's 15 seconds is held, and joined to the next request's first if that starts at once, or else taken as it is once the next request has been given 2.05 seconds of sound past it with no word, or starts its first word too late, or 20 seconds after its last word. A sentence asks something when it ends with a question mark, or starts, after a filler such as "so" or "um", with a question word (who, what, when, where, why, which, how), or with a word such as did, does, is, are, can or should followed by who or what it asks about ("did they", "is the", "can we"); it brings up a topic with "let's talk about", "tell me about" and the like, the topic being what follows; and it asks about the last time something happened with "the last time we", "remember when" and the like. Its key words are the words that aren't too common, a name or a number counting two, and a question or a recall needs two of them, or one name or number, to be asked about: "how are you" and "can you see my screen" aren't. Who said it makes no difference. A question isn't asked again within three minutes: two are the same when six tenths of the key words they hold between them are alike. Its card goes on top, three answer cards are kept, newest first, and their questions go to `gather_context` one at a time, oldest first, in plain words, for 12 passages of the notes and none of the transcripts, given up on after 10 seconds. On the owner's Mac, idle, a brief of 8 passages took 2.0 seconds, and 3.6 with the transcripts' passages, which a switch in `internal/live` turns on; in a call, 1.8 to 5.0 seconds. How long 12 take is still to be seen. A question is asked early, before its sentence is finished: as soon as the sentence being said would be noticed as a question, a topic or a recall if it ended there, it gets a card that isn't shown, whose question goes to `gather_context` as any card's does, after the shown ones. When the finished sentence asks the same, by the rule above, the card is shown with what was found for it, and On the Record isn't asked again; when the sentence goes on to ask something else, or nothing, the card is dropped, and its question, if still out, given up on. One is asked early at a time, and none that was asked in the last three minutes. From the brief, the lines of meetings' notes are read, each with the moment it cites, or its topic's, or else its meeting's link; what the board says about an item, documents and notebook entries aren't. A line scores each key word it has, a word of the same kin counting ("release" for "launch", "funding" for "money") as does a long word misheard by a letter or two, and half for each that only its topic, section or meeting has, and one and a half when it has the kind of answer the question wants: a sum for "how much" or a price, a number for "how many", a date or a day for "when" or a deadline, a name for "who". A line needs 2 to be shown, and six tenths of the best line's. At most three are shown, best first, one with a moment before one without, for a question about the last time the newest meeting's first, and of two as good the newer meeting's. Each says what was noted, cut to a line or two, and its meeting, date and moment, and a click opens that moment. When no line scores enough, the card says "No answer in the archive." rather than showing weak ones; from the moment its question is noticed until its answer comes it says "Looking…", and when asking fails, why. The thresholds are first guesses, in `internal/live`, to be tuned on the owner's meetings. A new recording starts with no cards. With [the local models](#the-local-models) chosen, those lines are kept for when the writing model can't answer, and the card shows its answer instead. [The panel](#the-panel) draws an answer card as this says, from when its question is noticed: its question, its word under it, and its answer a sentence a line with its sources as chips, or the lines that answer it.
- <a id="written-answers"></a>**Written answers.** With the local models chosen, the writing model first rewords each question for the search, before it goes to `gather_context`: it is asked for other words the notes may use for what the question asks about, at most 8, on one line ("capabilities, roadmap, plans, improvements" for features), and doesn't wait for its model to load. Go keeps, of its first line, the words of three to 24 letters that aren't too common or already the question's, 8 at most, and On the Record is asked the question as heard followed by them. When the model fails, is loading, or takes more than a second, or isn't free within a second of the question being noticed, the question goes as heard. On the owner's Mac it reworded made-up questions in 0.16 to 0.22 seconds. The brief's lines are then ranked by a score of their own: each key word of the question, or word the model added, that a line has counts whole, each that only its topic, section or meeting has counts half, and having the kind of answer the question wants one and a half, so that the lines under a heading that matches rank above those that share nothing; every line a browser can open, the same line once, those that score nothing included, below the rest, since an answer often shares no word with its question. They are kept, best first, until about 2,500 tokens (four characters a token), and numbered [1] to [n], each with its meeting, date, and what it sits under or the moment it was said. The writing model is asked for one or two short sentences, 40 words at most, answering the question from those lines only, the items of a list named briefly, the number of each line it used in square brackets after the fact, or exactly "Not in the archive" only if no line is about what the question asks, and told that notes may use other words for what is asked, such as capabilities or plans for features, and that a question about what someone wanted, planned or would like is answered by notes about their goals, plans, roadmap or future work; a topic brought up is asked as "What do the notes say about …?". Go reads what it writes: "Not in the archive", alone, is no direct answer; of anything else, those of its first two sentences that cite, and say something besides their citations, are kept, and they must cite only numbers it was given, put nothing else in brackets, give in digits only figures that a line they cite gives, in its text, what it sits under or its meeting's title, in digits or in words, or as its meeting's year or day of the month, so that "$750,000" may stand for a line's "$750k" and "$2M" for its "2,000,000", but "$2 million" not for its "$750k", and be 300 characters at most, and there must be one, or it is rejected. A figure it gives in words, such as "three things", isn't checked. With no direct answer, or one that is rejected, the card keeps the lines that share the question's words, as above, under "No direct answer; the closest notes:", since the model can be too strict when the notes use other words, and an answer it gets wrong says nothing about the notes; when no line shares them, it says "Not in the archive.", or "The local model's answer didn't check out, so it isn't shown.". An answer that checks out shows under its question, its citations numbered again from 1 in the order first cited, and under it each source as "[1] meeting · date · moment", from On Air's own copy of the line, a click opening that moment; "Reading what On the Record found…" shows meanwhile. The writing model does one thing at a time: a rewording first, then an answer, then a check, never while a round of typed questions is out. One answer is written at a time, oldest first, given up on after 8 seconds and a second, and only once its question is finished; one asked early waits for that. When the model is still loading, fails or is late, the card shows the lines that share the question's words, as above, and the status line says why.
- <a id="differs"></a>**What differs from the record.** With the local models chosen, each sentence said in the call that asks nothing and states a fact (a number, an amount, a date, a name, or a decision such as agreed, dropped or moved) is kept for a minute, and each line of a brief fetched for an answer or a card about a name that states one is kept for five, but for the notebook's. About every 15 seconds, only when a sentence that states a fact was said since the last check, or a brief came while one was in the minute, and never while a card, shown or asked early, waits for its answer, a question is reworded, or a round is out, the writing model is given the newest eight such sentences, numbered S1 to Sn, and the kept lines that share a key word with them, most first, twelve and about 900 tokens at most, numbered [1] to [n], and asked for JSON: whether a statement contradicts a line about the same thing, and if so the two numbers and the differing words of each. Go shows it only if the JSON says so with numbers it was given, the words quoted from each side are in it, and differ, the two share a key word, when the statement names a project, a person or a company the archive knows the line, what it sits under or its meeting names one of them too, or is a meeting the person named was in, by its title and its date, so that what was said of one company isn't checked against another's record, and the statement holds a fact, compared whole for a number, that the line doesn't; when only figures differ, one of the statement's must fit none of the line's, where a figure fits one that is the same, or, said roughly ("about", "over", "more than"), within half again of it, so that "past two thousand" fits 2,300. Then the panel shows, above the cards, "Differs from the record: said “…”; the record says “…”", both from On Air's own copies, with the line's meeting, date and moment, a click opening it, for two minutes or until another replaces it. One shows at a time, and a line shown to differ isn't asked about again for five minutes.
- <a id="the-panel"></a>**The panel.** A panel 340 points wide, at the top right of the display being recorded, or of the main display for a window, which floats above other windows and over full-screen apps and never takes focus. Under its title a quiet line says how many words were heard in the last minute and how many were the person's, or what went wrong. Then what was said that differs from the record, if anything, what the decision model said, and then three cards at most, newest on top, each in a quiet box: its title in 15-point type, then a short line a fact in 13.5-point, each with its sources as small rounded chips at its right, a click on one opening it in the browser, http or https only. The cards are the cards about names, the standing card, and the answer cards, from when their question is noticed; a card pushed off by newer ones doesn't come back. Go decides what each card says and which show; the Mac only draws them. The links to related meetings of before, and the line under the title on how the last question went, are no longer drawn. It shows while recording with Live Context on, and stays two minutes after the recording stops when it shows cards. It is marked `NSWindowSharingNone` to keep it out of other apps' captures, such as a call's screen share. While Live Context is on, On Air's own capture of a display leaves On Air itself out of the picture, so the panel isn't recorded, nor is the menu-bar dot; a window's recording never holds it anyway. A recording that started before Live Context was turned on doesn't leave it out, and the panel says so.
- **Asking a local model.** "Ask a local model about the call", under Show Live Context in the menu, is off until it is chosen, and remembered. It chooses both local models: the decision model's typed questions, here, and the writing model's [answers](#written-answers) and [checks](#differs). With it on, both start loading as listening starts. About every 10 seconds while recording, and only when something new was said, never while the writing model is at work or a card, shown or asked early, waits for its answer, On Air asks the decision model typed questions about the newest minute: is a decision being made, where it stands (proposed, agreed, taken back or none), and has the topic changed. A round that has no answer in 2 seconds is given up on. What clears a threshold shows in the panel above the cards for two minutes: "Decision: agreed, at 15:04." when a decision is at least 0.7 likely and its stance at least 0.5. A topic change at least 0.8 likely clears what the model said, and the links when they are asked for, and starts a fresh minute. The thresholds are in one place in `internal/live`, marked as first guesses to be tuned on the owner's own meetings. A model still loading is no failure: the status line says "The local model is loading." and the next round asks again. What goes wrong, such as a model slow or not set up, is said in the panel only.
- <a id="the-local-models"></a>**The local models.** The decision model is Liquid's d1-2B, the MLX 8-bit weights at a pinned revision, asked through mlx-vlm's `system_one`. The writing model is Qwen3 4B Instruct 2507, the MLX 4-bit weights from `mlx-community` at a pinned revision, 2.3 GB, under the Apache 2.0 licence, run through mlx-lm, greedy, with the prompt at most 4,096 tokens and what it writes at most 400. On Air runs a helper for each, a Python script built into the app, from the environment `tools/setup-models` makes in `Library/Application Support/On Air/models/venv` in the home folder. A helper speaks JSON lines over its standard input and output and opens no port. It reads its model from the Hugging Face cache with the Hub kept offline, downloads nothing, never trusts a repository's code, and refuses a snapshot that holds any Python file, so no code from the model's repository runs. On Air starts each as listening starts, which takes a few seconds while the model loads, starts it again if it dies, stops trying after three failures, answers in between or not, until the models are chosen again, and stops it when the models or Live Context are turned off, or by itself once it has gone half an hour unasked. It ends by itself when On Air does. The decision model's rounds don't wait for it to load; an answer waits for the writing model as long as its 8 seconds allow.
- **Apart from the recording.** The live side runs on goroutines and queues of its own. It hears what the recorder does through a tap on the recorder's edge, which passes each call on unchanged before the live side hears of it, and every input it takes returns at once: a field set, or a bounded queue of finished text and reports whose oldest go when it is full. A panic in it is recovered, and after three it stops, and the panel says so. What goes wrong on the live side is said only in the panel, never in the menu.

### How Live Context is checked

- **CI, on Linux.** Tests of whose each word is: the microphone more than twice as loud, the call's sound silent, a tie, both silent, a word with no length or outside what was measured, a result with no times, and a speaker's words in a row joined into one turn. Tests of the last minute (what it keeps, times that can't be right, both speakers in the order they spoke), of when to ask, of which links are picked and what each says, of links that can't be opened, of new links on top and none repeated within five minutes, of a late or abandoned answer, and of what the panel shows and says in each case. Tests of answers: questions, topics and recalls noticed, and what isn't one left alone; the kind of answer each wants; a result split into sentences at marks and pauses, with no times at marks only, and a sentence cut between two results joined, or ended by itself; the lines that answer five made-up questions picked from a made-up brief, a misheard name among them, and no weak line shown; one question asked at a time, oldest first, the same one not asked again within three minutes, an answer that fails, has nothing or never comes; and the controller answering a question it heard, with a diagnostic log of counts only. The controller is tested against a fake On the Record and a fake Mac: an answer shown, nothing asked when nothing new was said, an archive that is down, slow, never answers, panics or has no address, a panel that panics or hangs, and inputs that return at once and stay bounded whatever it does. The client is tested against a fake read-only door, reading a made-up brief in the shape learned from On the Record's code and asking `gather_context` in plain words and never for the screen, in the shapes learned from the door's `tools/list` and from On the Record's code: the answer read, the session started once and again when forgotten, JSON and event-stream answers, every error it can give and in good time, a door that isn't there, loopback only, no proxy and no redirect. The recorder's tests cover the setting. And one test records the same meeting without the live side and then with it failing in each of those ways, and finds every call the recorder made of the Mac, in order, and every file it left, the same each time.
- **Answers as they are said, in CI.** Tests of recognition's results taken a request at a time: a question finished once, 0.4 seconds after its mark, and not again when later results revise it; one with no mark after a pause; a sentence followed by more words, at a mark or a pause, finished at once; partial results with no times; a sentence cut between two requests joined, or taken once the next request has gone on without a word, or by time; a request whose final never came taken as last heard; and results with no request number standing alone, as finals did. Tests of questions asked early: kept for the finished question, with On the Record asked once and the answer written only then; dropped when the sentence asks something else or nothing, the question still out given up on; none for a question asked lately; and the controller asking before a question is finished and giving up on one that changed, with a log of counts only. Tests of rewording: what the model writes read strictly, and a question that goes as heard when the model is busy. And on a made-up brief of 39 lines from twelve passages, about 2,200 tokens, whose four lines under a heading on capabilities and the roadmap never say "feature": the ranking before kept 26 lines to 1,500 tokens and none of the four; the ranking now keeps all four, and puts them first once the model's words for the question count.
- **The local model, in CI,** against a fake helper: its answer read, an answer to an earlier question skipped, a slow answer given up on and the next one read, a helper that dies started again and then given up on, even when it answers before each death, one left unasked stopped by itself, one that can't load or never loads, a missing environment, and Close stopping it; the questions a round asks and the answers it reads. In `internal/live`: when a round is asked, what each verdict shows against the thresholds, a topic change clearing the panel, a model still loading said as such and asked again, a judge that is slow, never answers or panics, and a judge closed when it isn't chosen or Live Context is off. Against a fake writing model: the lines it is given, ranked, numbered and cut to the tokens allowed; answers read strictly (citations renumbered, two sentences kept, a number not given, no citation, citations alone, brackets with other things, a figure its lines don't give or only a line it doesn't cite gives, the same figure written otherwise, such as "$2M" for "2,000,000", "Not in the archive" alone or mixed in, too long, and the closest lines shown for no direct answer and for one rejected); checks read strictly (numbers not given, words it made up, a statement about something else, one that agrees, the same number in other words, a figure said roughly that fits and one that doesn't, not JSON), and figures read from digits and words; one difference shown at a time, for two minutes, the line not asked about again within five, and what was said before a brief came checked once it comes; the writer, the judge and the archive taking turns; a writer that fails, loads or is late leaving the lines that share the question's words; both models warmed as listening starts; and the controller writing an answer and finding a difference, with a log of counts only. The test of the recorder with the live side failing has a judge and a writer failing in each run too: slow, never answering, panicking and writing nonsense; and, with cards about names on, the archive's lists failing in the same ways as its answers, nonsense among them long, empty or strangely written, while the words said name someone the archive knows.

### What Live Context doesn't do yet

- A hosted model beside the local one (decision 9): jev isn't built. Nor are the other typed questions in docs/PRD.md, the brainstorm and review profiles, decisions that drift across meetings, speaker names, and the draft decision list.
- Show a count on the menu-bar dot when the panel is closed (decision 11), or look at the screen when it changes (decision 12): it listens only, asking the decision model about every 10 seconds and On the Record when a name or a question comes up.
- Know who is on a call: the standing card shows the open items of the archive's owners and of the people named in the call's first minute, not of the call's own list of people. Nor does it use `search`, or know a name that the archive's lists don't hold, such as a product only its notes name.
- Answer questions from the notebook, documents or the board: answers read meetings' notes only, though cards about names read the notebook's lines too. What is said is checked only against the lines of briefs fetched for answers and cards, never the notebook's.
- Close the panel by itself, other than by turning Live Context off.
- Follow a topic that isn't a name the archive's lists hold: a card comes for a project, a person or a company they hold, and an answer only for a question, or a topic brought up ("let's talk about …"), that names one, or a recall. Links to what the last minute connects to are no longer asked for. Whether the cards about names are useful in a real meeting is still to be seen: they were tuned on a replay only.

## The hand-over core

[`core/handover`](core/handover) decides each step of handing a finished recording to the folder another program watches: copy it there under a hidden name, compare the copy with the original, give it its real name without replacing anything, and remove the original once its time to keep it has passed. It touches no file itself. Its caller carries out each step and reports what happened, so a hand-over can be interrupted at any step and carried on from its record. It is its own Go module.

Invariant's factory built it from [#1](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/1), and it merged through [#3](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/pull/3):

- 42 statements, ratified on the issue and pinned by hash in [`ratified.lock`](core/handover/.invariant/ratified.lock): 18 invariants, 13 witnesses, 5 known bugs, 4 fairness conditions, 1 liveness property and the spec.
- A TLA+ model, [`RecordingHandover.tla`](core/handover/.invariant/specs/RecordingHandover.tla), which TLC explores exhaustively within `MaxAttempts = 2`, `MaxCleanupAttempts = 2` and `MaxDifferences = 2`: 7,296 distinct states to depth 60, no violation and no deadlock, all 13 witnesses reached, the liveness property holding, and all 5 known bugs caught. One size larger, the code reaches the model's states again: 32,955 of them.
- Go code that Gobra proves against its contracts (10 of 10 functions), and that is tested against the model: 247,592 steps recorded, all 7,296 model states visited.
- A second review of its explorer, the code that walks the model's states, left two notes on the pull request, which aren't taken up yet: `MaxDifferences` is missing from the manifest's parameters, and a refused step's returned record isn't observed.

The gate verifies it again on every pull request. The app reaches it as the owner chose in [#11](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/11): it requires the core's tagged release, `github.com/gitdek/onair/core/handover v0.1.0`, pinned by hash in `go.sum`. The tag `core/handover/v0.1.0` is on `dec76f5`, where the gate had verified the core unchanged since [#3](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/pull/3) merged it, so the app builds against the code that was verified. A change to the core needs a new tag, and the app moves to it in a change of its own. [`internal/delivery`](internal/delivery) is its caller, so rules 5, 6, 7 and 11 are held by the app through it: proved in the core for its decisions, and tested on real folders for the steps.

## The session core

[`core/session`](core/session) decides a recording's life: at most one recording at a time, a second Start refused and said; every recording that starts ends exactly once, handed over or kept on disk with its error shown, and how it ended is shown even if On Air dies first; a recording cut short by a crash carries on, up to a set number of restarts, counting a restart that may not have happened. It was built by the factory from [#8](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/8) and finished by hand in [#21](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/pull/21), where Gobra's proofs needed a loop in place of a slice copy and a lighter helper for finding a running recording; no contract changed.

- 49 statements, ratified on the issue and pinned by hash in [`ratified.lock`](core/session/.invariant/ratified.lock).
- A TLA+ model that TLC explores exhaustively: 39,742 states, every witness reached, every planted bug caught, both liveness properties holding under their fairness.
- Go code proved with Gobra against contracts that restate the model, 29 of 29 functions with overflow checks, and checked against the model step by step. The check one size larger isn't claimed: TLC doesn't finish it within its three minutes.

Nothing in the app calls it yet. Today's recorder in `internal/recorder` still decides these things in plain Go, and wiring the core in is part of what remains of milestone one.

## The pieces core

[`core/pieces`](core/pieces) keeps a recording's pieces: every closed piece keeps its bytes until the joined recording has been handed over and three days have passed; a piece counts as whole only once its bytes are flushed and it has its final name; numbers are never reused; a join uses every closed piece in order, leaves out one that can't be read and records how long the gap is, and saves that before the joined file gets its name; a join cut short by a crash is tried again at launch, up to a limit on interruptions that a retry doesn't reset; a hand-over cut short by a crash is asked for again at launch with the same recording's identity, and the hand-over core takes it only once, as the owner chose on the issue; and every error names where the recording's pieces are. It was built by the factory from [#9](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/issues/9) and finished by hand in [#24](https://github.com/gitdek/onair/pull/24), where Gobra didn't finish within its 20 minutes: its helpers now state each piece's facts where they hold its permission and count over one copy of the pieces, two contracts had a clause moved ahead of the one it needed, and two ghost counts were rewritten with the same values. No contract's meaning changed.

- 68 statements, ratified on the issue and pinned by hash in [`ratified.lock`](core/pieces/.invariant/ratified.lock).
- A TLA+ model that TLC explores exhaustively: 24,760 states, every witness reached, every planted bug caught, all five liveness properties holding under their fairness, and the check one size larger passes too.
- Go code proved with Gobra against contracts that restate the model, 31 of 31 functions with overflow checks, and checked against the model step by step.

Nothing in the app calls it yet. Today's `internal/spool` and `internal/recorder` still keep the pieces in plain Go, and wiring the core in is part of what remains of milestone one.
